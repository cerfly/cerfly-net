Right now I'm reading four books that keep orbiting the same idea: that less — less stuff, less noise, less optional work — can be the point. They've started to talk to each other, so let me introduce the conversation.

*Four Thousand Weeks* (Oliver Burkeman) is the anti-productivity book. It sounds like a time-management manual and is the opposite: no system ever clears your inbox, and the real skill is accepting which things you'll miss. More useful to me than any trick it could have taught.

*How to Simplify Your Life* (Tiki Kustenmacher & Lothar Seiwert) is a checklist in disguise — seven areas of life, each with concrete things to let go of. The habit it left me with is a question I now ask about almost everything: does this need to be this complicated?

*The Power of Habit* (Charles Duhigg) made me see my own loops. Every habit is cue → routine → reward, and you rarely destroy one; you replace it. Naming the cue is what lets me catch the routine before it fires.

*Early Retirement Extreme* (Jacob Lund Fisker) is the sharpest of the four. The core claim is simple and humbling: your savings rate matters more than your income. Treat your life as a system to redesign, not a budget to endure.

They're not a reading list with a unifying thesis — they're just where my head is lately. The working list, with the full short reviews, lives on my [Now page](/now.html).
